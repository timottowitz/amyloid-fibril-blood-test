1. The Diagnostic Paradox: Normal D-Dimer with Severe Capillary Hypoperfusion

Millions of patients suffering from chronic post-viral fatigue, Long COVID, Post-Vaccination Syndrome, and ME/CFS face identical diagnostic invalidation: routine laboratory blood tests—including D-Dimer, Erythrocyte Sedimentation Rate (ESR), and high-sensitivity C-Reactive Protein (CRP)—return completely normal, despite severe physical exhaustion and brain fog.

Groundbreaking research published by Prof. Etheresia Pretorius and Prof. Douglas B. Kell resolves this paradox: persistent symptoms are driven by anomalous amyloid fibrils that are chemically and structurally distinct from normal blood clots.

2. Why Thioflavin T (ThT) Fluorescence Staining Is Mandatory

Because biochemical assays cannot quantify insoluble amyloid structures, direct biophysical visualization via specific amyloid fluorophores is required:

  • Quaternary Intercalation: Thioflavin T (ThT) is a benzothiazole fluorophore. In aqueous solution, its benzyl and benzothiazole rings rotate freely around a central C-C bond, quenching fluorescence. When ThT molecules intercalate into the continuous surface grooves of amyloid cross-beta sheets, their rotational mobility is locked.
  • 1,000-Fold Fluorescence Quantum Spike: Rotational arrest triggers an instantaneous surge in fluorescence emission at ~535 nm under 450–485 nm excitation.
  • Platelet Entanglement: Dual-staining using magenta CD62P (P-selectin) markers demonstrates that amyloid fibrils trigger massive localized platelet hyperactivation.

3. Clinical Staging Criteria for Amyloid Fibril Accumulation

  1. Stage 1 (Minimal): Dispersed micro-aggregates (<5 µm), low background activation.
  2. Stage 2 (Moderate): Dense clustered amyloid fibrils (5–20 µm) with early platelet hyperactivation.
  3. Stage 3 (Significant): Widespread branching fibrinaloid sheets (20–50 µm) with extensive platelet clumping.
  4. Stage 4 (Severe): Massive confluent amyloid conglomerates (>50 µm) causing extensive field occlusion.
