## Executive Summary & Pathophysiological Mechanism

Patients presenting with chronic fatigue, cognitive dysfunction ("brain fog"), and exercise intolerance following viral exposure (Long COVID, Post-Vaccination Syndrome, ME/CFS) routinely show **normal findings on standard coagulation and inflammation panels** (D-Dimer, ESR, CRP, PT/INR).

Extensive research led by Prof. Etheresia Pretorius and Prof. Douglas B. Kell demonstrates that the underlying driver of tissue hypoperfusion is **anomalous amyloid fibrils**—insoluble, cross-beta sheet microclots formed through non-enzymatic misfolding of circulating fibrinogen.

## 1. Microvascular Terminal Capillary Occlusion

Normal human capillaries measure 6 to 8 micrometers in diameter. Healthy erythrocytes (red blood cells) must deform flexibly to transit individual capillary loops. When anomalous amyloid fibrils circulate, they physically trap erythrocytes, block terminal flow, and cause chronic downstream tissue hypoxia.

## 2. Fluorophore Binding Mechanics: Why Thioflavin T (ThT) Is Mandatory

Biochemical assays fail to detect amyloid fibrils because the clots do not generate soluble cleavage epitopes. Definitive laboratory visualization requires **Thioflavin T (ThT)** fluorophore intercalation:

* **Quaternary Intercalation:** Free ThT in solution displays negligible fluorescence due to free internal rotation around its central C–C bond. When ThT molecules intercalate into the continuous surface grooves of amyloid cross-beta sheets, rotation is immobilized.
* **Fluorescent Quantum Spike:** Rotational arrest generates an instantaneous 1,000-fold surge in green-gold fluorescence (excitation $\sim 450\text{--}485\text{ nm}$, emission peak at $\sim 535\text{ nm}$).
* **Dual-Staining Visualization:** When paired with platelet activation markers (e.g., CD62P / P-selectin in magenta), fluorescence microscopy reveals massive platelet hyperactivation surrounding amyloid fibrils.

## 3. Laboratory Staging Criteria (Stages 1 to 4)

1. **Stage 1 (Minimal):** Sparse, tiny micro-aggregates ($<5\ \mu\text{m}$) scattered across fields. Minimal platelet clumping.
2. **Stage 2 (Moderate):** Clustered amyloid fibrils ($5\text{--}20\ \mu\text{m}$) with localized platelet aggregation.
3. **Stage 3 (Significant):** Extensive branching amyloid fibril sheets ($20\text{--}50\ \mu\text{m}$) with widespread platelet clumping and microvascular occlusion potential.
4. **Stage 4 (Severe):** Massive confluent amyloid aggregates ($>50\ \mu\text{m}$), dense matted fibril networks occluding multiple fields of view.
